Franchise Agreement

📄 Your Franchise Agreement

Your signed Franchise Agreement is the foundational legal document for your MILO Insulation franchise. It outlines your rights, obligations, territory, fees, and the terms of your partnership with MILO.

📁 Access Your Agreement: Your franchise agreement is available in your Documents folder within this portal. Please review it carefully and keep a copy for your records. Contact Corporate Support with any questions.

LLC / Business Entity Formation

🏛️ Business Entity Checklist

You must form a legal business entity (typically an LLC) to operate your MILO franchise. Complete each step below:

Choose your business entity type
Most franchisees form an LLC (Limited Liability Company). Consult with your attorney or accountant to determine the best structure for your situation.
Select and register your business name
Your business name should include "MILO Insulation" as required by the Franchise Agreement (e.g., "MILO Insulation of [Your City], LLC").
File articles of organization with your state
File with your state's Secretary of State office. Filing fees and timelines vary by state.
Create an Operating Agreement
Even for single-member LLCs, an operating agreement is recommended. It defines ownership structure and operating procedures.
Obtain a Registered Agent
Required in most states. You can serve as your own or use a registered agent service.

EIN Registration

🔢 Employer Identification Number (EIN)

Your EIN is your business's federal tax ID number. You'll need it for banking, taxes, hiring, and vendor accounts.

Apply for your EIN with the IRS
Apply online at irs.gov — it's free and you'll receive your EIN immediately. You'll need your LLC formation documents.
Save your EIN confirmation letter
Keep a copy of your IRS EIN confirmation (Form CP 575) for your records. You'll need it for bank account setup.

Required Insurance

🛡️ Insurance Coverage Requirements

All MILO franchisees must maintain the following insurance policies. Obtain quotes and bind coverage before beginning operations.

General Liability Insurance
Covers third-party bodily injury and property damage claims. Minimum recommended: $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate.
Workers' Compensation Insurance
Required in most states once you hire employees. Covers workplace injuries and illness. Check your state requirements — some states require coverage even for the business owner.
Commercial Auto Insurance
Required for any vehicles used in business operations (service vans, trucks, etc.). Covers liability and physical damage.
Professional Liability Insurance
Also called Errors & Omissions (E&O). Covers claims related to your professional services, advice, or workmanship.
💡 Tip: Shop insurance through a commercial insurance broker who has experience with contractor/insulation businesses. Ask about bundled policies (BOP — Business Owner's Policy) to save on premiums.

State & Local Licensing

📋 Licensing Requirements Checklist

Licensing requirements vary by state and municipality. Research and complete all applicable items:

State contractor's license
Many states require a general contractor or specialty insulation contractor license. Check your state licensing board requirements.
Local business license / permit
Contact your city or county clerk's office for local business license requirements.
State sales tax registration
Register with your state's Department of Revenue to collect and remit sales tax if applicable.
Home improvement contractor registration
Some states require specific registration for home improvement contractors. Verify with your state.
EPA Lead-Safe Certification (if applicable)
Required if working on homes built before 1978. Obtain through EPA-accredited training providers.

Business Bank Account

🏦 Bank Account Setup

Open a dedicated business checking account to keep personal and business finances separate. You'll need:

Open a business checking account
Bring your EIN confirmation, LLC formation documents, and operating agreement to a local bank or credit union.
Set up online banking and bill pay
Enable online access for managing payments, transfers, and tracking expenses.
Order business debit card and/or credit card
A business credit card helps build business credit and track expenses by category.
Set up accounting software
QuickBooks Online is recommended. Connect your bank account for automatic transaction tracking.
